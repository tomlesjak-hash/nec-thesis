# Section 4. Novelties of the Proposed Topic

*Draft 1, 2026-09-11. Approximately 600 words. Reference numbers match the list in Section 1.*

---

The principal novelty is a comparison that does not exist. No published study puts four structurally distinct regime-switching processes side by side as the gate of a neural mixture of experts, on the cross-section of equity returns, with expert capacity matched and one walk-forward protocol throughout. Each clause does work. *Structurally distinct* separates the study from the comparisons already present in the mixture-of-experts literature, which vary configuration inside a fixed routing paradigm [21, 22, 23] rather than varying what defines a state. *As the gate* separates it from switching state-space models and Markovian recurrent networks [12, 13], in which the regime governs the predictor rather than a mixing weight. *Cross-section of equity returns* separates it from the volatility and single-index work that dominates the recent regime-gated literature [5, 10]. And *matched capacity and common protocol* is what makes it a controlled comparison instead of a bake-off, which the existing single-mechanism studies do not provide.

Two more specific novelties follow from the choice of mechanisms. Statistical jump models have been used for financial regime identification and for asset allocation [18, 19] but have not, as far as can be established, been used as the gate of a mixture of experts; the same applies to Wasserstein distributional clustering [20]. Both are therefore first applications, not variations. The jump model is the more consequential: its switching penalty makes persistence directly tunable, so the comparison can isolate what persistence contributes instead of inferring it from models that differ in several respects at once.

A third contribution is a directional hypothesis stated in advance. The closest existing study reports its largest advantage during sustained high-volatility periods and its smallest in the windows surrounding regime transitions [5], which is the behaviour expected of a gate built from rolling statistics: such a gate reports where the market currently is, not whether it is about to leave. Whether a gate carrying transition dynamics reverses that pattern is a testable prediction with a pre-specified window definition, and the answer is informative in either direction.

Several smaller results arise from the architecture, because the comparison requires the specification to be fixed first and the literature does not supply it. Expert depth has never been ablated inside a mixture of experts for cross-sectional return prediction: the closest precedent fixes two hidden layers without testing the choice, and the standalone-predictor studies that do vary depth confound it with capacity, since each added layer also adds parameters, with the consequence that the best-known of them cannot separate any pair of depths from one to five hidden layers in its own significance tests [27]. Running every mechanism at every candidate depth at matched total capacity, and reporting the resulting grid rather than a tuned selection from it, therefore produces a table that does not currently exist for any target: no published study reports regime mechanisms against expert capacity. It is obtained from work the study has to do in any case, and it is informative whether or not an interaction appears, since a stable ordering across depths is the robustness evidence the main comparison rests on. Two construction configurations are likewise absent from the published record, a jointly trained base paired with zero-initialised residual experts, and the residual model with its correction penalty removed, so the pilot that settles the construction fills those cells as well.
