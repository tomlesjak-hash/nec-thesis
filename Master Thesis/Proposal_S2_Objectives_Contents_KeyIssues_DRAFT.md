## Research objectives

The study asks whether the mechanism that generates a gating signal, rather than the presence of gating itself, affects the predictive performance of a mixture-of-experts model on the cross-section of equity returns. Existing regime-aware expert mixtures compute their mixing weights as a learned function of instantaneous features, and the first aim is to determine whether replacing that function with a regime-switching process carrying explicit transition dynamics and persistence changes out-of-sample accuracy, ranking performance and training reliability, with expert capacity, input features and evaluation protocol held fixed. The second aim is to identify which structural property is responsible for any difference that emerges: four mechanism families are compared, classical Markov switching, penalty-based statistical jump models, covariate-driven time-varying transition probabilities, and model-free distributional clustering, and because they disagree about what defines a regime, a ranking across them attributes any advantage to a specific property, not to regime-awareness in general. The third aim is to locate any advantage in time. The closest existing study reports its largest gains during sustained high-volatility periods and its smallest during regime-transition windows [5], and the complementary hypothesis, that a gate carrying transition dynamics performs relatively better precisely in those windows, is registered in advance with a pre-specified window definition. A fourth aim is the comparison framework itself: a capacity-matched, protocol-matched setting in which regime mechanisms can be evaluated against one another on this task, released so that further mechanisms can be added without rebuilding the baseline.

## Main contents

The work has four parts. The first is a library of regime mechanisms behind a common interface, each mapping a date to a vector of mixing weights, covering the four families above plus two reference gates, a clustering assignment with no temporal structure, which isolates the value of the Markov assumption, and a smooth-transition gate on a single observable threshold variable, which provides an interpretable floor. The second is a residual mixture-of-experts predictor for the cross-section of returns, built on a panel of firm characteristics, into which any of these gates can be substituted without other changes. The third is a construction experiment that fixes the architectural configuration before the mechanisms are compared, since two of the relevant architectural cells are untested in the published literature and cannot be settled by citation. The fourth is the comparison itself, with diagnostics covering the size and timing of each gate's contribution, its dispersion across experts, how fitted regime weights line up with recession dates and volatility quantiles, and performance broken down by market condition.

## Key issues to be solved

The central difficulty is measurement, not prediction. In a jointly trained mixture the shared and routed components exchange capacity during optimisation, so the aggregate expert contribution ceases to be a clean measure of what the gate supplied, and an architecture that isolates that contribution must be adopted and its cost quantified, not assumed. Isolating the term is not the same as identifying its content, however: the corrections remain unrestricted functions of the same inputs the base receives, so an underfitted base would simply be patched by the experts, and experts that converged on a common function would leave the routing decorative while the correction remained large. Establishing that the correction measures what it is claimed to measure therefore requires diagnostics designed for the purpose, since none follows from the architecture. A second difficulty is that the mechanisms are not interchangeable as components: two of the four are differentiable and could in principle be trained jointly with the predictor, while the other two can enter only as a pre-computed state sequence, so any comparison must choose between using each mechanism in its natural form and holding the training procedure fixed. A third is that the number of regimes cannot be selected by standard likelihood-ratio methods, the transition probabilities being unidentified nuisance parameters under the null of a single regime [24], so the number must be fixed by convention and its influence established separately. A fourth is that the architecture of the experts themselves is not established. The choice of a multilayer perceptron follows from the input being a point-in-time vector of firm characteristics, not a price history, and from the requirement that experts must not model temporal structure themselves, since experts that could would absorb the very variation the gate is meant to supply. How deep those perceptrons should be is a different matter: the closest precedent fixes two hidden layers without testing the choice, and the standalone-predictor literature, which does vary depth, places the optimum at three or four hidden layers without being able to distinguish between them, or indeed between those and the shallower alternatives, since its own significance tests separate no pair of depths [27]. No study ablates expert depth inside a mixture for this task at all. The specification therefore has to be established, not cited, and, since a study far larger than this one could not separate depths on accuracy, it has to be settled on training stability and on whether the configuration leaves a measurable expert contribution, and not on predictive accuracy alone. A further difficulty is that the appropriate depth may not be the same for every gate, since the concentration of a gate's weights determines how much data each expert effectively trains on; fixing one depth for all would then handicap some mechanisms, while allowing it to vary raises the question of whether the mechanisms are still being compared like with like. The resolution adopted is to fix neither and report the full grid of mechanisms against depths, which shows the interaction instead of hiding it, at a cost in computation that is the main reason the experts must remain small. A fifth is that the training-stability criterion used in the volatility literature is a QLIKE threshold, undefined for a signed return forecast [5], leaving no established convention for the target studied here. A sixth is the scope of the regime state: the closest precedent supplies both a market-wide and a stock-level conditioning variable [5], whereas a market-only gate makes the object of study a market regime process, not a per-stock routing rule, and which is appropriate is not settled by the literature. Underlying all of these is the weakness of the signal itself, out-of-sample *R*² on the order of 0.4 per cent at the monthly horizon [27], which makes the differences under test small relative to the variation induced by random initialisation, and places the burden of the study on evaluation design as much as on modelling.
